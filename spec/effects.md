## 1. Doctrine

- **The tracked effect is observable interaction with the world.** Reading input, writing output, consulting a clock or an entropy source, mutating foreign state. Nothing else is an effect: allocation is not, `var` inside a function body is not (it cannot escape — Statements §6.2), and **throwing is not**. Exceptions are the partiality/defect channel (Exceptions spec), the Rust cut: `Int.div` throwing `DivideByZeroError` does not make division effectful, and `Result` is untouched. Without this cut the doctrine of throwing companions would make the whole prelude impure.
- **The lattice has two points: pure and impure.** There are no effect rows, no effect families, no user-declared effect kinds. A design that wants "which effect" is a different language; Hexagon asks only "does the world notice".
- **Purity is the silent one.** A bare call, an unmarked body, a `->` arrow — silence is the strongest claim. Effects are what get spelled, and **one alphabet spells them in both places**: the marks `!` and `?` ride the call they describe and the arrow they colour alike. A reader who sees nothing may assume the world is untouched.
- **The system is Hindley–Milner all the way down.** The effect is one more unifiable component of every function type, ranging over the two-point lattice; effect variables are ordinary type variables (§3.4). This is the effects system reached by *refusing to leave* HM — the same commitment that fixed the rest of the type system. There is no subeffecting, no row polymorphism, no effect subsumption: colours unify or they do not. One seat compares them instead *(#867)*: an instance meeting a constraint member's contract, where every arrow the header writes is a bound — a pure body may stand under an effectful header, and an instance may accept more than the header promises (§13). That is the whole exception — at calls, at demands, at data fields, and on every implementation's own written face, colours still unify or they do not.
- **Asynchrony is out of scope, permanently for this ruling.** `async` is a separate axis, not a point on this lattice; v1 is synchronous, and the async question files its own issue when it arises. Nothing here pre-decides it.

## 2. The three arrows

Function types carry a colour on every arrow. The display grammar (zero/one/many domains, right association) is Functions §5.1's, unchanged; this section fixes what each arrow means.

```text
->     pure: this function touches nothing the world can observe
->?    linked: this signature's one effect variable — the caller's instantiation decides
->!    the impure constant: this function performs effects, unconditionally
```

**There is one arrow, and the colour is a mark on it.** The three spellings differ only in the mark they carry, and the marks are the call trichotomy's own (§3.1) — `f(x)` / `f?(x)` / `f!(x)` against `->` / `->?` / `->!`, the same three symbols answering the same three-valued question. §3.3 makes the correspondence exact: a call's mark reports the colour of the outermost arrow of the callee's type at that instantiation, so `save!(document)` asserts precisely that `save`'s outer arrow is `->!` here. A reader learns the alphabet once.

The consequence for the *type* grammar is that the fat arrow `=>` is not one of its tokens. `=>` is a term-level arrow only — the lambda's and the `match`/`catch` arm's (Lexer §8.1) — and Functions §4.1's return-annotation slot needs no parenthesization rule as a result (§2.6).

It also settles a collision inside the display itself. Under the predecessor spelling a constrained higher-order face used one symbol for two unrelated jobs in a single line — the arrows, and a constraint separator borrowed from Haskell:

```text
Show a => (Seq(a), (a) => Unit) => Unit       -- predecessor: three `=>`, two meanings
<a: Show> (Seq(a), (a) ->? Unit) ->? Unit     -- now: no `=>` at all
```

The arrows gave up `=>` because they never needed it, and the separator went with them *(#410)*: a displayed scheme now spells its constraints source-shaped, with §4.2's binder bracket as a quantifier prefix (Functions §5.1). So `=>` appears nowhere in a displayed type, and the token has exactly one reading language-wide — the term-level one.

### 2.1 `->` — the pure arrow

A `->` function performs no observable effect at any instantiation. As a demand — a `->` arrow in a parameter annotation — it refuses impure arguments by ordinary unification (§4.3): `memoize`-shaped functions get their purity guarantee from the type, with no new mechanism.

### 2.2 `->?` — the linked arrow

Every `->?` written in one signature denotes **the same, single, implicitly quantified effect variable** — the signature's colour. One variable per signature, never two (§2.4) — and a local header or annotation with no inlet of its own borrows that variable rather than quantifying one (§2.2.2). The linked reading is what makes higher-order functions effect-polymorphic for free:

```text
fold : (Seq(a), b, (b, a) ->? b) ->? b
```

`fold`'s callback arrow and every other `->?` in its signature — here, its own outer arrow — share one colour. The outer arrow is linked because `fold`'s body is a conduit: its `?` call on the callback (§3.1) joins the body's own colour to the signature's variable, so running `fold` is exactly as effectful as the callback makes it. Instantiated with a pure `combine`, the whole call is pure and bare; instantiated impure, the call is impure and wears `!` (§3.3, §4.1). The signature never says "impure"; it says "as impure as you make it".

The mark is the call mark, and it reads as the call mark reads: *this arrow defers to my caller.* One `?` inside a body and one `?` on an arrow name the same variable — in `fold`, both signature arrows and the body's `combine?(accumulator, value)` are three occurrences of one colour.

#### 2.2.1 Where `->?` is legal — the inlet rule

`->?` is legal **only where a variable can be owned — by the signature the arrow stands in, through that signature's own inlet, or, in a local type position inside a body, by the nearest enclosing signature that can lend one (§2.2.2's lexical ownership rule).** Anywhere else it is an error (§4.4); it is never given a second reading.

An **inlet** is an occurrence of the signature's colour inside a **parameter type of any arrow on the signature's application spine** — the chain of arrows reached from the signature's root by descending through results — **at any nesting depth and at any polarity within that parameter type**. Depth and polarity are irrelevant because the caller supplies the whole argument value and therefore pins every colour inside it — the test is syntactic, so an occurrence beneath a phantom constructor parameter counts too, though no argument value carries it and nothing pins the colour there (§13.2) — at whichever application step it supplies it: `(step: () ->? String) -> Int`, `(pair: (Int ->? Int, Int)) -> Int`, and `(f: (Int ->? Int) -> Int) -> Int` all have an inlet; so does a `->?` inside a record *type* standing in a parameter annotation — `(handler: { step: () ->? String }) -> Int`; and so does `() -> ((Int ->? Int) ->? Int)`, whose inlet the caller reaches at the **second** application — a curried signature is applied step by step, and each step's arguments are supplied by a caller. The occurrence walk is §3.4's, which already counts the effect slot as a component at the arrow's own sign; the inlet test reads that walk and asks only whether some spine arrow's parameter type contains the variable — sign and depth discarded, because a supplied argument pins what it contains however deeply and in whatever position.

A signature with no inlet has a variable no caller can pin: **the caller pins exactly what it supplies, and an occurrence it only receives pins nothing.** Where no enclosing signature can lend one (§2.2.2's lexical ownership rule — a local header or annotation under an inlet-bearing signature borrows instead of quantifying), that is the error. Two face shapes take it on that ground, and two positions take it for having no signature at all:

- an **outer-only face** — `(seed: String) ->? Int`: the variable is only a spine arrow's own colour, and no supplied argument contains it. A spine arrow's colour is not an inlet; only occurrences inside a parameter type are. Nothing instantiates it, so the callee would have to satisfy every colour at once — not a claim a body can meet;
- a **received-only face** — `(): Int -> (Int ->? Int)`: the variable stands only in what every application hands *back*. The refusal is of the *written* face: a `->?` there names the signature's one variable and promises a callee that meets every colour at once. Inference is under no such constraint, and it can generalize a variable that stands only off the application spine — `let mk() = (1, defer)` returns a tuple whose function component keeps `defer`'s generalized colour, `() -> (Int, (() ->? Unit) -> (() ->? Unit))` — a face that displays under §10's licence and cannot be written back. §10's lone-variable paragraph is the ruling for the paste: the display is right about the colour, and §4.4 explains the refusal. What the refusal costs is that annotation, not the program *(#873)*;
- a **data-declaration field** (§2.5) — a `record` or `union` has no signature at all, so there is no variable to link to;
- a **`type` alias body** (Declarations Preamble §4) — likewise a fragment with no enclosing quantifier.

A `->?` in *parameter* position is always linked, even when it is the signature's only one: `store(callback: () ->? String): Int` is polymorphic in `callback`'s colour and accepts pure and impure arguments alike — the variable, if nothing ever observes it, simply generalizes unconstrained (§3.4). It is never rounded up to the constant, which is §2.4's own rationale applied. `store`'s own arrow, which nothing in its body constrains either, is pure: `store : (() ->? String) -> Int` *(#868 — §3.4's defaulting)*. The parameter's variable is preserved because a caller supplies it; the outer colour is defaulted because nobody does.

The rule's shape is deliberately the call side's. A `?` at a call with nothing to conduct is an error (§4.1) rather than a call quietly re-read as `!`; a `->?` on an arrow with nothing to link is an error for the same reason and with the same sentence behind it. **One spelling, one meaning, everywhere it is legal** — and where the meaning is unavailable, a diagnostic rather than a substitute.

**A local face is not a signature under this rule** *(#873)*. Inside a body, a binding's type may carry an enclosing signature's variable — in `outer(action: () ->? Unit)`'s body, `let g = action`, `let h = () => action?()`, `let h(): Unit = action?()`, and `fun h(): Unit = action?()` all have the type `() ->? Unit` — and that face is a **monotype fragment of the enclosing scope**, not an outer-only signature: the variable is `outer`'s, it is not generalized at the local binding (§3.4's capture bullet; Functions §8 — a variable that belongs to the environment is never the inner binding's to quantify), and whoever pins the local binding pins `outer`'s callback. Colour scope is lexical. §2.2.2 spells the written form, §3.1 and §3.4 say what a `?` on such a binding means, and §10 displays the face undecorated where an inlet-less `->?` at the display location would name that same variable, numbering it and naming its owner where one would not.

#### 2.2.2 Local type positions

Inside a body, a `->?` written in a **local type position** — a binding annotation, an ascription, a local declaration's header, or a lambda's own parameter and return annotations — with no inlet of its own denotes **the variable of the nearest enclosing signature that can own one**: `let h: () ->? String = g` and `let h = (g : () ->? String)` are the same claim, and both link. This is the **lexical ownership rule**, and it is one rule for every written `->?`: a function type that carries an inlet of its own owns its variable; an inlet-less `->?` borrows the nearest enclosing signature that can own one; where there is none, it is §2.2.1's error *(#873)*. The written side and the inferred side differ here on purpose: inference reports *whichever* variable a callee carries (§3.1), while a written inlet-less `->?` names *the nearest* owner — so the display numbers a captured variable wherever an undecorated paste at that location would name a different owner, and tooling names the owner (§10). The legality condition is the enclosing signature's own (§2.2.1) — a local `->?` is legal exactly where the nearest enclosing signature that can own a variable admits one, and it never *supplies* the inlet it needs: inlets are the signature's, not the body's.

Two boundaries keep this exact:

- **A local function type that carries its own inlet is its own signature.** `let f: (Tx ->? a) ->! a = …` quantifies its own colour, exactly as before; only an inlet-less local `->?` reaches out to the enclosing signature. The variable a fragment names is the nearest enclosing signature that can own one. Ownership attaches to a whole annotation, ascription, or header — the arrows on its application spine and the parameter types they carry, together; no function type nested inside one owns a variable of its own, whether it stands in a parameter type (`(f: (Int ->? Int) -> Int) -> Int` is one signature with one colour, §2.2.1) or in a data component off that spine — a nested result component is never independently quantified, and the inlet definition is §2.2.1's unchanged. The second half is why `let mk3(): (Int, (() ->? Unit) -> (() ->? Unit))` stays unwritable wherever §2.2.1's received-only refusal reaches it — at module level, and in a body no inlet-bearing signature encloses — even though the tuple component it returns would carry an inlet on its own: the spine ends at the tuple, and no arrow a caller applies supplies the colour. Under an inlet-bearing signature the same header borrows instead, by this bullet's last sentence. A **local declaration's header** — a `let h(…)` or `fun h(…)` nested in a body — is the same fragment under the same rule *(#873)*: with an inlet among its parameters it **owns** its variable, `fun h(cb: () ->? Int): Int` denoting `cb`'s own colour as a module-level header would — owning is not always quantifying: a body that also absorbs a captured colour joins the two, and the joined variable belongs to the enclosing environment (§3.4); without one, a `->?` in its return annotation borrows — the `->?` in `fun h(): () ->? Unit = () => action?()` inside `outer(action: () ->? Unit)` is `outer`'s variable, exactly as it is in `let h: () ->? Unit = () => action?()`. The same holds of a lambda literal's own annotations: `(cb: () ->? Int) => cb?()` carries its own inlet and is its own signature, while `(): () ->? Int => …` under an inlet-bearing signature borrows. A header is refused for want of an inlet only where no enclosing signature can lend one: at module level, and in a body that no inlet-bearing signature encloses.
- **Where there is no signature at all**, §4.4's own clause says so: the annotation is not part of any function signature. The clause is about shape and doctrine, not merely position. A binding annotation that is itself a **function type** is a signature *wherever it stands* — a module-level `let f: (Tx ->? a) ->! a = …` quantifies its own colour exactly as a local one does, and an inlet-less module-level function-type annotation takes §2.2.1's *signature* clause, not this one. What takes the no-signature clause is a `->?` inside a **non-function-type** annotation with no enclosing signature (a module-level `let` or `var` whose annotation is not a function type — shape decides for a `var` exactly as for a `let`), and any **`extern let`** annotation — an extern `let` declares a foreign *value*, not a callable row (FFI Part 4 §4.5's posture; the callable form with a signature of its own is `extern fun`).

The ascription's rigid purity demand (§2.6, the Ascription spec) is unchanged: `(f : a -> b)` still demands the pure constant. What this section fixes is the linked spelling's meaning in that seat, which previously diverged between the ascribed and annotated forms of one intent.

### 2.3 `->!` — the impure constant

`->!` is one token (Lexer §8.1); the bang trails the arrow it condemns, matching the call mark's postfix position. `!->` is not a token and cannot become one — the mark trails, on arrows as at calls (Lexer §8.2).

`->!` names the impure constant, and it is the **only** spelling of it. Every position whose colour is constantly impure writes it: a function that performs its own unconditional effects (§2.4's join), a data field that pulls the world (§2.5), an inlet-less face (§2.2.1's refusals), a boundary row that writes it (§6.1). There is no position where the constant may be left implicit and no second spelling that coincides with it — the abolition of the else-constant rule (§2.2.1) is exactly the removal of that coincidence.

One kind of position reads the spelling as a bound rather than a claim *(#867)*: an arrow a constraint member's header writes is a **contract** — a ceiling where the caller invokes it, a floor where the caller supplies it — and `->!` on the member's outer arrow licenses an instance to perform effects without asserting that any instance does (§13.1). The constant is unchanged — it is still the top of the lattice, still the mark every call through the member wears — and what differs is only that the seat honoring the contract compares against it rather than unifying with it (§13.2).

### 2.4 The conservative join — one variable, ever

A body that both performs its own unconditional effects and forwards a callback's colour does **not** get a second effect variable. Its own colour is the conservative join: constant-impure — counting impure is fine, and the join is what `->!` spells.

**The join governs the function's *own* colour only.** The callback arrows it forwards **keep their linked variable**:

```text
withTransaction : (String ->? String) ->! String
```

The outer arrow is `->!` — `withTransaction` writes to the world on its own account, so every call to it wears `!` (§3.3). The callback's arrow stays `->?` — a *pure* callback is accepted, and stays pure in the caller's accounting. Rounding the callback's arrow up to `->!` would refuse pure callbacks outright: purity-as-polymorphism works through variables, and `->!` is not one.

The callback's `->?` is also this signature's inlet (§2.2.1), which is what keeps the face legal: a signature may carry a linked arrow while its own outer arrow is a constant, and `withTransaction` is the everyday shape that does.

### 2.5 Data-field arrows: the constants only

A function-typed field of a `record` or `union` declaration carries `->` (a pure field) or `->!` (an impure one). **A data declaration has no signature, so its arrows have no variable to link to** — and `->?` there is therefore the §2.2.1 error, with the fixit `->!`.

The position still matters, but it now selects between *legal* and *rejected* rather than between two meanings. `{ step: () ->? String }` **in a parameter annotation** is part of that function's signature, so its arrow links and supplies an inlet — the enclosing function is polymorphic in the field's colour. The identical text **in a `record` declaration** is data, and is refused. A record type is a set of values with no enclosing quantifier; a signature is a quantified contract. Both outcomes are pinned in the conformance suite.

That is the whole of the old divergence: no written shape reads two ways any more, and the reader who learns "`->?` means my caller chooses" never meets a position that quietly means something else.

This is the sentence `Stream` stands on: `Stream(a)`'s field `next: () ->! Option(a)` is pull-impure by declaration (`stream.md`), and `Seq(a)`'s field `pull: () -> Option((a, Seq(a)))` is pure by declaration — the two-type split of §7.

### 2.6 Values wear no colours

- **Lambdas are always written with the term arrow `=>`**, pure ones included. The term-level `=>` is the lambda syntax (Functions §3.1) and says nothing about colour; a lambda's colour is **inferred from its body's marks**. There is no pure-asserting term arrow.
- **References are colourless.** A name, a field access, a stored function — none carries a mark (§3.2's corollary: no argument list, no mark). Storing an impure function is not an effect; calling it is.
- A **term-level purity demand** is the existing ascription: `(f : a -> b)` — rigid, per the Ascription spec. A **signature demand** (`->` in a parameter annotation) refuses impure arguments by unification. No new demand form exists. A term-level `->?` in an ascription is §2.2.2's local position: it names the nearest enclosing signature that can own a variable, under that signature's own legality.
- **The two levels no longer share a token, and a lambda return annotation therefore needs no parenthesization rule.** `=>` is a term arrow and the `->` family is a type arrow; the annotation grammar can be right-associative and greedy without hazard, because the token that ends it cannot be one of its own:

  ```text
  (x): A ->! B => body      -- annotation `A ->! B`, body `body`
  (x): a => y => x          -- annotation `a`, body `y => x` — the curried lambda, unchanged
  ```

  Both parse unambiguously and mean what they read as. The predecessor of this bullet gave an unparenthesized `=>` in that slot to the body and required `(x): (A => B) => body`; that rule, its diagnostic, and the diagnostic-suppression machinery it needed are all withdrawn — the ambiguity they managed was token overloading and no longer exists. Functions §4.1 owns the annotation grammar.

## 3. The call trichotomy

### 3.1 Three marks, one question

Every call wears exactly one of three states:

```text
f(x)      bare — this call is pure
f!(x)     dirty — this call performs effects
f?(x)     conducting — this call is as effectful as the enclosing instantiation makes it
```

The teaching model: **every call is a pipe.** A pipe is clean, dirty, or conducting. The developer's question at each call: *is this a source, or just a conduit?* A function that reads the world is a source — its calls wear `!`. A function that merely runs what it was handed conducts — its call on the handed-in value wears `?`, and the conduit is only live when the far end is: `combine?(accumulator, value)` inside `fold` runs effects exactly when the caller instantiated `combine` impure. References are colourless as a corollary, not a fence: no pipe, no mark.

**The marks are the arrows' marks** (§2). A call's mark and the callee's arrow mark are the same symbol reporting the same colour, and §3.3 is what makes that identity exact rather than mnemonic. The three questions a reader ever asks — of an arrow, of a call — have one alphabet of answers.

The trichotomy is exhaustive because a mark reads the **callee's** colour at this instantiation and asks one question of it: pure, the impure constant, or a variable. *Which* variable is not the mark's business. Inside a body, more than one non-constant colour can be in scope — the body's own signature's variable, where it has one, and the variable of every enclosing signature reached by capture, because colour scope is **lexical** *(#873)* — and `?` reports the callee's inferred variable whichever it is: `action?()` conducts `outer`'s colour from `outer(action: () ->? Unit)`'s own body and from a lambda, a local `let`, or a nested `fun` inside it alike. The `?` neither selects the nearest signature's own variable nor unifies the callee's with it; what it joins is the *body's own colour* (§3.4's conduit arm — at a constraint seat it bounds that colour instead, §13.2), which is how a nested body becomes a conduit of a colour its own header never wrote, and why a nested helper that conducts a captured colour stays polymorphic in a callback of its own it does not itself conduct (§3.4). One-variable-per-signature (§2.2) is a fact about what one written signature spells, and it stands; it was never a bound on how many variables a body can see. The one apparent exception — a body storing a lambda into a function-typed field — is resolved by §2.5: a data field's arrow is a constant (and `->?` there is refused outright), so no second variable enters scope that way.

A `?` call is legal where there is a variable for it to report: the callee's colour is the variable of some signature in scope — the body's own, or an enclosing one's reached by capture. A body that no inlet-bearing signature encloses has no variable in scope, and §4.1's table is what refuses the `?` there.

`?` is the deliberate pun on Rust's trained gesture: this line defers to my caller.

### 3.2 The mark's anchor is the argument list

A mark governs **an argument list**, whatever the callee expression:

```text
save!(document)          -- ordinary call
(source.step)!()         -- parenthesized field access, then a marked call
document.show()          -- a dot call marks its own argument list (bare here: show is pure)
stream.next!()           -- dot call, marked
maker("s")!(document)    -- each argument list marks its own call
```

- The mark is written glued on both sides — against the callee expression it follows and against the `(` it governs (Lexer §8.1). A floating mark reads as an operator, and no such operator exists: `readLine ! ()`, `readLine! ()`, and `readLine !()` all take §9's mark-position error. Dot calls (Method Syntax §2.1) and the `(e.name)(…)` opt-out each anchor the mark to their own list; a chain marks each link for what that link's call is.
- A **pipe stage** is a call, so it takes a mark: `x |> save!` — the bare-stage form's mark stands glued at the end of the stage, and the rewrite carries it onto the call it builds: `save!(x)`. Operators §8 owns the rewrite; a stage with its own argument list marks that list as usual (`x |> take!(3)` → `take!(x, 3)`).
- A mark anywhere else — on a reference, mid-expression — is a parse error (§9): a reference carries no colour.
- A dot call whose goal defers to its owner region's deadline (Method Syntax §3.1) anchors its mark to the same argument list, read off whatever colour the deadline produces. A goal that falls to the imposed row (Method Syntax §3.5) is a pure call: the imposed row's arrow is written `->` there, and a row is data (§2.5) — so a mark on such a call is refused like any other mark on a pure call, and the impure reading needs an annotation on the receiver.

### 3.3 The outermost arrow: a mark describes this call only

**A mark speaks about the colour of the outermost arrow of the callee's type, at this instantiation — nothing more.** A call whose *evaluation* touches nothing is bare, whatever it returns:

```text
let wired = compose(save2, audit2)    -- bare: evaluating compose builds a closure
wired!(document)                      -- the composite's own invocation is the effect
compose(save2, audit2)!(document)     -- both in one expression: outer call bare, inner list marked
```

Evaluating `compose` allocates a closure and touches nothing — its body is neither a source nor a conduit, so its own colour is unconstrained and therefore pure (§3.4's third arm) — the call is bare even when the composite it returns is impure. The effect surfaces where the composite is *invoked*, and the mark surfaces with it. Read it as order of operations for marks: each argument list is marked for the arrow it discharges.

The example holds in every body, inlet-bearing ones included *(#868)*: constructing a closure is not invoking it, and a colour nothing in `compose`'s body constrains is pure (§3.4's third arm). The same holds of every closure builder — `defer(action)`, `Stream.map(randoms, double)` (`stream.md` §4.2) — and of the function that returns one:

```text
let defer(action: () ->? Unit) = () => action?()
defer : (() ->? Unit) -> (() ->? Unit)      -- the outer arrow pure; the callback's colour preserved through to the closure's
```

`?` names a preserved dependency and nothing else; the rule that once read an undetermined call inside an inlet-bearing body as a conduit is withdrawn (§11).

### 3.4 Effect variables are type variables

The effect component rides the ordinary machinery — unification, levels, generalization (Functions §8) — with no parallel solver:

- **Monomorphic in the knot.** Within a `fun` block's strongly-connected component, a signature's effect variable is a not-yet-generalized monotype like every other variable (Functions §7.4); recursive calls share it. It generalizes per member, when that member's binding generalizes.
- **Settled at body close.** A declaration's own colour and its calls' mark obligations are resolved when its body closes, not at module end — a `fun` knot's members excepted, whose own colours and sibling-call obligations alike wait for the knot's close (the knot bullet below, #868). This is required, not latitude: under end-of-module settling, a module-internal call to a not-yet-generalized neighbour would see a still-linked variable, and the pure corpus would read as conduits — every bare call in `Seq.hex` would demand `?`.
- **A body's own colour is solved by three arms, in order.** A body that absorbs an impure-constant call is a **source**: its own colour is the impure constant (and if its written face is `->`, that is §4.2's pure-face error, at the offending call). A body whose `?` call absorbs a variable — its own signature's, or an enclosing signature's reached by capture (§3.1) — is a **conduit** of it: its own colour *unifies with* that variable — this is how one-variable-per-signature emerges rather than being imposed, why `fold`'s outer arrow is linked (§2.2), and why a nested `fun h(): Unit = action?()` in `outer(action: () ->? Unit)`'s body has the colour `outer`'s callback has, `h : () ->? Unit`, a face `outer`'s body calls with `?` (§2.2.1's local-face paragraph). Two variables one body absorbs become one, whichever signatures own them — `fun h(cb: () ->? Int): Int` calling both `action?()` and `cb?()` joins `h`'s variable to `outer`'s, and inside `outer` that `h` is no longer polymorphic in `cb`'s colour — a concrete callback handed to that `h` pins `outer`'s variable, and either colour it pins is a report at that pin: a pure one is the pure-direction report, an impure one the impure-direction report placed as the pure direction places its own — primary at the pin, a label at each written `->?` the variable spells, one report for the two signatures that share it (§4.2) — while a variable the body never absorbs is untouched: the same `h` calling only `action?()` keeps `cb`'s variable its own, generalized per call, displayed `(() ->?¹ Int) ->?² Int` (§10), `h?(pure)` and `h?(impure)` both legal in `outer`'s body. A body that is neither has an **unconstrained** colour, and it **defaults to pure before generalization, whatever the signature's inlets** *(#868)* — after the body's dependencies are resolved (its callees settled, its `?` calls joined) and before the binding's scheme is built: at body close for a lone binding, at the knot's close for a `fun` block member, and for a lambda that is no binding's right-hand side, where the binding whose body holds it closes; at an instance body's constraint seat the defaulting is held back until the seat has compared and settled what the contract fixed — after which a colour the seat bounded below and released takes the join of those bounds, a fifth dependency the defaulting never touches, so the defaulting reaches only a colour the seat left unbounded (§13.2) — or failed, after which it runs as it would outside a seat (§13.2) — because a slot the contract bounds below is not a colour nothing claimed, and at that seat a call on a freshened callback slot bounds the calling body's colour below instead of unifying with it, the conduit arm's one qualification (§13.2). Nothing observed the colour, so pure is what it is: constructing a closure that will run a callback is not running it. `store : (() ->? String) -> Int`; `defer : (() ->? Unit) -> (() ->? Unit)`, the returned closure's arrow linked to the callback's through the body's `?` call and the outer arrow pure.
- **What the defaulting never touches is a dependency**, and four things are dependencies outside a constraint seat, where a fifth joins them (the arm above; §13.2): a signature parameter's variable (next bullet); a body's own colour once a `?` call has joined it — to its own signature's variable or to a captured one; a body's own colour once a **written `->?`** has joined it — a `->?` on the outer arrow of a binding annotation that is itself a function type — its own signature where it carries an inlet, the enclosing signature's fragment where a local one does not (§2.2.2) — links the colour to that signature's variable exactly as a `?` call does, so `let f: (() ->? Unit) ->? Int = (action) => 1` keeps the face it writes rather than defaulting under it (a written `->` or `->!` joins nothing: it is checked against the solved colour, §4.2); and a knot member's own colour once a sibling call has joined it (the knot bullet). The linked faces are what the language exists to keep; the defaulting reaches only what nothing has claimed.
- **A captured colour never generalizes at the binding that captures it** *(#873)*. Levels decide (Functions §8): an enclosing signature's variable belongs to the enclosing body's environment, so a nested binding whose face carries it quantifies its own variables and leaves that one free — `fun h(): Unit = action?()` has a scheme with no effect variable of its own. Pinning the nested binding therefore pins the enclosing signature: `let p: () -> Unit = h` in `outer`'s body solves `outer`'s callback pure, and the report is §4.2's pure-direction report, at that pin, naming `outer`'s written `->?`. What a nested face shows is the enclosing variable itself — not a fresh copy, and not a signature of `h`'s own; §10 decides whether the display decorates it.
- **The defaulting clause, at calls.** A call's colour still undetermined when marks are checked defaults to **pure** (bare), in every body *(#868)* — except on a **knot sibling's colour, which the clause never pins**: that colour is a live monotype another member's body will solve, and both its value and the call's mark obligation wait for the knot's close (next bullet). Everywhere else a callee's undetermined colour is a fresh instantiation of a generalized unconstrained variable, which nothing binds, and pinning it pure weakens no face because no face depended on it — the enclosing body's own colour is defaulted by the arm above on the same ground. A **signature parameter's** effect variable is never defaulted by either clause: it generalizes with the binding like any type variable, which is exactly what keeps `store(callback: () ->? String)` polymorphic (§2.2).
- **Knot colours and obligations settle at the knot's close** *(#868)*. Within a `fun` block's strongly-connected component a sibling's colour is a not-yet-generalized monotype (the first bullet), so a call to a sibling may meet a colour that is undetermined now and solved by a body not yet checked. The colour is left as it is, and the call's mark obligation is **recorded and settled when the knot closes**, never at the caller's body close, and never by conducting or by pinning because the colour was unsolved at the time. The knot's close runs the arms **over the whole component, stratified, to a fixpoint** before anything defaults — the source arm first, to its own fixpoint: a member whose sibling call absorbs a source is a source in turn, and so on until no member changes, each such member taking §2.4's join — its own colour the impure constant, its inlets untouched, since the join governs a member's own colour only and an inlet already pinned stays pinned; then the conduit arm over what remains: a member whose sibling call absorbs a colour the knot has joined to a signature variable is a conduit of it; then the colours still unconstrained default pure; and only then are the recorded obligations read. Source before conduit is what makes the answer independent of the members' order — a member that is both reached by a source and reaching a conduit is a source, never a conduit whose inlet the source would constantify. The close needs no re-inference: it is a closure over the sibling-call edges the bodies already recorded. `a(cb: () ->? Unit) = b(cb)` beside `b(cb: () ->? Unit): Int = if True then 1 else a(cb)` — one component, each member calling the other — is two bare calls and two pure faces, `(() ->? Unit) -> Int` each; `even`/`odd` passing a callback down and calling it at the base are conduits, `?` on the sibling calls as on the callback, because the knot's colour joined the signature's — and so is a member that never calls the callback itself but calls the sibling that does; a sibling that turns out to be a source makes every member that calls it a source in turn — every sibling call to it `!`, the caller's own face `->!` — however early that member's body closed. A sibling whose colour a caller's body pinned pure by ordinary unification (a `->` demand the sibling was handed to as a value) and that the source arm then claims is §4.3's refusal, reported at that demand: the demand is what the report names as violated, the arm's constant is the face the sibling keeps (`->!`, as hover shows it), and the knot's close is where the two are compared. Generalization is still per member at its SCC (Functions §7.3, §7.4, §8); only the *timing* of the sibling colours and obligations moves, and only to where the knot's colours exist to read. A knot nested in a body captures as a lone binding does *(#873)*: a member whose `?` call absorbs an enclosing signature's variable is a conduit of it, the conduit arm counts that variable as a signature variable the knot has joined, and every member whose sibling call reaches it conducts it in turn — in `outer(action: () ->? Unit)`'s body, a knot whose `b(n: Int): Int` calls `action?()` and then returns `n`, beside `a(n: Int): Int = if n == 0 then b?(0) else a?(n - 1)`, gives both members `outer`'s colour, `Int ->? Int` each, and `outer`'s own call `a?(3)` wears `?`. A module-level knot has no enclosing signature and captures nothing.
- **Expected types carry the slot too** (#513). Functions §4.3's expected-type propagation moves a seat's unifications ahead of body inference, and an expectation's arrows arrive with their colours as ordinary components — nothing colour-specific is added or bypassed: a lambda's own colour is still inferred from its body (§2.6), face checks (§4.2) and mark computation (§4.1) read what they always read, and propagation reports nothing of its own. One seat is excepted *(#867)*: at a constraint member's honor seat the expectation's colours are replaced by fresh variables before propagation, because §13.2 compares them afterwards instead of unifying them.
- **Every occurrence walk counts the effect slot.** Analyses that walk a function type's components — declaration-site variance foremost — treat the effect slot as one more component, **at the arrow's own sign**: a colour is a fact about invoking the function, which is what the result position already means, so a parameter arrow's colour is contravariant with the parameter arrow. Skipping the slot makes every effect variable read as absent, and a variable the walk never sees is not one the type's quantification reaches (`decisions-ml-dialect-generalization-2026-08.md` §4.1), and one seen only through a constructor takes that constructor's claim — invariant by default at a bare opaque parameter (that document's §6.2) and where a compiler-known constructor supplies no row (its §5.3) — which pins faces monomorphic that the arms above worked to keep polymorphic. §2.2.1's inlet test is a second client of this walk, and reads it at its coarsest: *does some application-spine arrow's parameter type contain the variable* — sign and depth discarded, because a supplied argument pins what it contains however deeply and in whatever position.

## 4. Enforcement is symmetric, and error-grade

### 4.1 At calls: six directions, one-token fixits

The required mark at a call is computed from the callee's outermost arrow colour at this instantiation: impure constant → `!`; a variable — the body's own signature's, or one captured from an enclosing signature (§3.1) → `?`; pure → bare. A colour still undetermined when the mark is checked is pure — inside an inlet-bearing body as anywhere (§3.4's defaulting clause, #868) — and a knot sibling's is checked at the knot's close, when it is no longer undetermined. **Any other mark is an error — including a mark on a provably pure call** (the two suppressions are a §4.4 recovery's, which binds nothing, and a failed constraint seat's, which suppresses the reports §13.2 names). A tolerated-but-wrong mark would rot into noise; symmetric enforcement is what keeps silence meaningful. All six wrong-mark directions report the same shape, each with a one-token fixit:

> this call runs effects, so `save` wants `!`, not no mark
> this call is as effectful as the enclosing instantiation makes it, so `combine` wants `?`, not `!`
> this call is pure, so `next` wants no mark, not `?`

(the remaining three directions permute the same sentence; §9 tabulates.)

### 4.2 At faces: both directions

This section governs an implementation's **own written face** — a return annotation, a parameter's arrow, an ascription (Functions §4.1; the Ascription spec). A constraint member header is a **contract**, not the face of the instance body that honors it — an `honor` member's, a default's, or a `widens` door's: none of the four bullets below fires against the contract, a pure body under a `->!` or `->?` member being neither the third nor the fourth bullet's error, and §13.2 owns that seat *(#867)*. A face such a body writes for itself — a member's optional annotation, a door's own signature, an ascription inside it — is checked here as any face is.

A written arrow that contradicts the body's solved colour is an error **in both directions**:

- `->` over a body that performs effects: reported at the offending call — *"this call performs effects, and the enclosing function's face is the pure arrow `->` — a pure face cannot run effects"* — the span that names which call broke the promise.
- `->?` over a body solved to the impure constant: *"this signature's `->?` promises a colour the caller chooses, but the body solves it to the impure constant — a function that performs its own unconditional effects rounds up, and its face is `->!`"* — fixit `->!` (§2.4's join, surfaced) — where the impure colour instead arrives at a pin, the report and its fixit take the pin's shape (below).
- `->?` over a body that pins the variable pure — through a `->` demand it hands the callback to, say — *"…but the body solves it to the pure constant — the honest face is `->`"* — fixit `->`. This is the **lie of generality**, mechanized: a pure function wearing `->?` would force `!`-or-`?` ceremony onto every caller for effects that cannot occur. A body that merely leaves the variable unconstrained does not fire it: the written `->?` joins the colour (§3.4), and the face is what it writes. The report's primary span is the **pin** — the demand, annotation, or supplied argument whose unification solved the variable pure — with a **label** at every written `->?` the solved variable spells and one fixit whose edits rewrite each of them to `->` *(#873)*: one report however many signatures share the variable, because through capture a nested header and its enclosing signature can spell one variable (§3.4), and one act contradicts both contracts. This is the pinning-use placement Functions §8 already gives a monomorphic type variable's conflicting use.
- `->!` over a body that performs no unconditional effect: when the body conducts, *"this face is the impure constant `->!`, but the body performs no unconditional effect — it is effect-polymorphic, and its face is `->?`"* — fixit `->?`; when the body's colour is pure, *"…but the body performs no effect — its face is `->`"* — fixit `->` *(#868: a pure body is pure, not polymorphic; the advice names the face the body has)*.

The pure direction matters as much as the impure one: over-claiming and under-claiming both break the reader's contract, and neither is a style matter.

The impure-direction report stands **at the signature's written `->?`**, not presumptively at the outer arrow: the constantified variable may be spelled only on a nested arrow — `(h: ((Int) ->? Int) -> Int)` over a body that pins `h`'s callback impure — while the outer arrow is honestly `->` or `->!`. The advice never tells the writer to change an arrow that is not there. The two directions place their primary differently for a reason *(#873)*: the ordinary impure-direction body performs its effect in a call of its own, and the one signature that lies is that body's, so the arrow is the report's seat; a pure-direction solve is always an act of unification at a pin, which can contradict several signatures' arrows at once, so the pin is the seat and the arrows are its labels. Where an impure solve is itself a pin — a concrete impure callback handed to a helper whose variable is captured (§3.4), a `->?` callback handed to a `->!` field (§2.5's constant field — §4.4's last paragraph names the same report) — it places as the pure direction does: primary at the pin, a label at every written `->?` the solved variable spells — occurrences of the variable actually pinned, never of another variable however near — one report however many signatures share it, and one fixit of the pure direction's shape, rewriting each labelled `->?` to `->!`. There is no join to preserve there: the colour that arrived is the inlets' own variable, not a constant the body raised beside them, so an outer-only rewrite would leave the same pin contradicting the re-linked inlets. In both directions the arrows named are exactly the ones that spell the variable.

**Where the body's own call sourced the constant, the impure direction's fixit repairs the arrow that lied, and the join decides which that is** (§2.4). When the *outer* arrow is among the written `->?` occurrences, the honest repair is the join itself: the outer arrow alone becomes `->!`, and the signature's inlets keep their `->?` — they re-link as the constant-outer signature's variable, which is exactly `withTransaction`'s face, and rewriting them too would refuse the pure callbacks §2.4 exists to keep. Only when the outer arrow is *not* a written `->?` are the nested occurrences the condemned colour's whole spelling, and the fixit then rewrites each of them. The pure direction has no join to preserve: every written `->?` becomes `->`.

### 4.3 The pure demand

An impure function meeting a `->` demand is an ordinary unification failure with a dedicated report:

> a `->` arrow promises purity, and this function performs effects — the demand is written `->`, the function's face `->?` or `->!`

This is the whole enforcement of `memoize`-class contracts and of `Seq`'s §7 posture; nothing beyond unification is involved. A declared pattern's `view` is one more seat of the demand, and it writes no `->` — the head carries no arrow — so it takes a clause of its own: "a pattern's `view` is run by matching, so it is pure — the demand is the pattern head's, and this function's face is `->?` or `->!`" (Pattern Declarations §2.1, §5).

The failure has a reverse direction, and it needs its own sentence: a *pure* function refused where the impure constant is demanded — a `->!` data field (§2.5), an inlet-less face, any written `->!`. The report above speaks of a written `->` *demand* in every clause, and in the reverse direction the demand wrote no `->` — each clause misdescribes the program. The reverse report says what is actually true:

> this position's arrow is the impure constant — its colour is fixed where the type is declared, and this function's face is the pure `->`; the demand cannot weaken — change the position's declared arrow, or supply the effectful function the position promises

The constraint seat is neither direction of this section *(#867)*: an instance under a member's contract is **compared**, not demanded (§13.2), so a pure body under a `->!` member passes exactly where a pure function at a `->!` field is refused. A field, a parameter arrow outside a contract, a written `->!` in an annotation — these are *types*, and a type unifies; a member header is a *contract*, and a contract is a bound.

### 4.4 At `->?`: the inlet rule, enforced

A `->?` written where §2.2.1 does not admit one is an error at the arrow, never a re-reading of it. The report names the position's reason and offers the constant, because the constant is what the writer of a data field or an inlet-less face almost always meant:

> `->?` is the caller's colour, and this position has no caller to choose it — a `record` field is data, not a signature; write `->!` for a function that pulls the world, or `->` for one that does not

The refusing positions each get their own middle clause — a `union` field says `union`, a `type` alias says *"an alias is a type fragment, not a signature"*, an inlet-less signature (an outer-only or received-only face where no enclosing signature can lend the variable — a module-level declaration, a constraint member header, an extern callable row, or a local header or annotation in a body no inlet-bearing signature encloses; §2.2.1) says *"nothing a caller of this signature supplies carries `->?`, so nothing instantiates it"*, and a type position outside any function signature (a module-level binding annotation, an `extern let`, a module-level `var` — §2.2.2) says *"this annotation is not part of a function signature"*. §9 tabulates all five. The fixit is `->!` in every case; a writer who wanted purity reaches for `->` without prompting, and a writer who wanted polymorphism has to add the parameter that would supply it — which is the design conversation the error exists to start.

A refused `->?` recovers as a **marked colour**: it reads locally as the impure constant — hover shows `->!`, and mark obligations and face checks whose colour traces to a §4.4 recovery are suppressed — and at unification it behaves as the error type does, absorbing whatever it meets and binding nothing (next paragraph). The one report is the ruling; the constant is scaffolding that keeps the rest of the body checkable, not a claim to re-litigate downstream.

**The recovery binds nothing it meets** *(#873)*. Unified with a variable, the recovered colour leaves the variable as it was — an enclosing signature's colour, a captured callback's, a knot sibling's — so no face, colour, or mark outside the refused position changes on the recovery's account, and only what traces to the recovered colour itself is suppressed. Under the previous reading the constant flowed outward: a refused `record` field `f: () ->? Unit` recovered as `() ->! Unit`, and a body that handed its own `->?` callback to that record's constructor then met the recovered constant, and `outer`'s own written `->?` was solved to it. Suppressed by the recovery's own clause, that is `outer`'s face silently rewritten to `(() ->! Unit) -> …` with no report anywhere near it; unsuppressed, it is §4.2's impure-direction report on a signature that did nothing wrong — exactly the report the same body draws against a genuine `->!` field. Neither is a repair the writer can act on. Scaffolding holds up the refused position and nothing beyond it.

## 5. Calls with no mark seat

Four call forms have no position for a mark, by grammar:

1. **Operator applications** — `x + y` elaborates to a constraint member call (Operators §1.1) with no bang position; `x +! y` is not grammar, and negation is the word `not`.
2. **Bracket indexing** — `xs[i]` is `at`, definitionally (Collections Part 3 §5).
3. **`for` heads** — `for x in xs` desugars through the iteration protocol (Loops §2.3, §7.1).
4. **String interpolation** — `"${x}"` renders its holes through `Show` (Primitive Types §5).

The consequence is a demand, not an accident: **everything these forms dispatch to must be pure.**

- **The members these forms reach are the prelude's, and every prelude member's contract is `->`** *(narrowed by #867 from a rule about every constraint; §13.5)*. Operators elaborate to `Num`, `Signed`, `Frac`, `Pow`, `Eq`, `Ord`, `Concat`, and `Integral` members, `for` heads to `Iterable.toSeq`, interpolation to `Show.show` — by identity, never by spelling — and every member of every prelude constraint writes `->` on its outer arrow and, as standard-library policy, on every arrow beneath it (Constraints §7 lists them); brackets elaborate to a compiler-known lookup rather than a member — `Vector`'s and `String`'s `at`, a companion operation the bracket form demands pure (Collections Part 3 §5, §9; §4.3), and `Map`'s keyed read over `Hash` (Collections Part 4 §4.1), pure by construction. That is a **compile-breaking standard-library constraint**, of the kind the prelude seat order already is: a prelude header written `->!` would put an unmarkable call on an effectful member, and the conformance suite is to pin each header. An `honor` body under a `->` contract must solve pure — §13.2's seat check, the mechanism behind Constraints §2's sentence — and derived instances are pure by construction (their generated bodies call members). A *user* constraint may declare an effectful member (§13); no unmarkable form reaches one, because no unmarkable form reaches a user constraint.
- **`Iterable` can never have an effectful instance.** `toSeq`'s contract is `->`, so every instance's body must solve pure; a type whose traversal performs effects cannot honor `Iterable` and cannot stand in a `for` head. This lands exactly where it will matter next: `Map`/`Set` iteration is pure by this sentence.
- **`for` headers are never marked, and loop bodies may be impure.** The head is protocol (pure by the above); the body is a block, not a lambda, and its statements mark their own calls as usual (ruling: iteration protocol is pure; effects live in the body).

## 6. The world's doors

### 6.1 The extern ownership split

Purity at a boundary declaration splits by **who owns the implementation**, and every boundary row writes the arrow the split is about:

- **Every callable boundary row writes its arrow** *(#869)* — `->`, `->!`, or `->?`, between its parameters and its result, as a constraint member header does (§13.1): a row is a contract with no body to infer from. FFI Part 4 §4.5 owns the form and the three readings at the boundary; what this section owns is who answers for the arrow.
- **Compiler-owned intrinsic rows** (`extern from "hex:intrinsic"`) have their arrows **verified**: the compiler is the implementer and is held to the declared scheme, parametricity obligation included (Intrinsics §4.2). Nearly every prelude intrinsic row writes `->`; the two guarded property reads beneath `JsError.message` and `JsError.stack` write `->!` (FFI Part 11 §7). That is an inventory, not a rule of this section: an intrinsic row may write any of the three, and each is verified — vacuously at `->!`.
- **User-written rows have their arrows trusted.** A foreign function is trust territory (FFI Part 1 §3.1). `->!` is the honest arrow for the unknown, and the join for a row that touches the world on its own account (§2.4); `->` is the **trusted purity claim** — believed, never checked, the module author answering for it, sound only under §6.2's species or for a function that genuinely computes; `->?` is the **declared conduit** — one colour variable at the outer arrow and at every `->?` the signature writes, an ordinary linked face under the inlet rule (§2.2.1), with §3.3 and §3.4 unchanged at the call. A row unwilling to claim purity or a dependency writes `->!`. *(The impure default and the contextual `pure`/`conduit` modifiers this section once described are retired; the arrows say what they said.)*

### 6.2 The trusted-purity species

A `->` on a boundary row — user-written or intrinsic — whose implementation does touch the world is unsound, except for three shapes where the claim is *sound*, not merely customary. The ruling names them so every future claim can be tested against one of them:

- **Species (a): unobservable world-writes.** A write-only channel the program cannot read back — never a foreign property write: a `set` row is the write capability it grants and is declared `->!` regardless (FFI Part 5 §4.1, #869), because a property the foreign side can read is not write-only from the world's point of view. The debug probe is the member: a pure-faced `log` that writes to the console changes nothing any Hexagon expression can observe. What it forfeits is multiplicity and ordering — under the per-instantiation contract a probe in a pure producer may print once, many times, or never (a memoized `Seq` step prints once however often traversed). Fine for debugging; disqualifying for real logging, which belongs behind a banged extern. **The caveat:** `console.log` is replaceable, and a replaced sink is a read path; a conforming debug probe captures its sink at initialization.
- **Species (b): owned, memoized, at-most-once world-reads.** A read the runtime performs at most once and then owns — the result is a value; the world can no longer vary it. `seqMemoize` is the member, and so is FFI Part 3's inbound adapter: *memoization makes the foreign iterator's mutability invisible* (Part 3 §4) — the launder that lets an effectful JavaScript iterable become a pure-faced `Seq` at the boundary, unchanged in v1. The hash-table placement mix (`hashTrieMix`, #365) is the third member: its lowering reads the per-process placement seed (Collections Part 2 §2.4) at most once and owns it thereafter, so placement is a value function for the life of the process — which is precisely the within-execution iteration-order determinism §2.4 promises and nothing more.
- **Species (c): reads of stable data** *(#869)*. A pure collection denotes stable contents, and a read of foreign data that cannot vary while Hexagon holds it is a read of a value: contents **captured at acquisition** — the snapshot semantics `Array` (#876; FFI Part 2 §6.2) and `JsMap`/`JsSet` (#875; FFI Part 10 §2) have under FFI Part 1 §2.2 and §5.4, whose `length`/`size`/element reads are then reads of a value — or data a **stability contract** binds the foreign side to hold still for as long as Hexagon may observe it, of the shape the boundary collections carried before #876 and #875 (recorded at FFI Part 2 §13.2 and FFI Part 10 §14), which no boundary collection now carries, whose violation is an FFI Part 1 §3.1 contract violation like any other (a user row's `->` over foreign data, FFI Part 4 §4.5; a `->` getter, FFI Part 5 §3.1, is such a claim and is tested against exactly this: which contract holds the data still). What makes the face sound is the stability of the data, never a rule that forbids the compiler to share the read: a replayable read of data foreign code may vary is `->!`, which is where `JsError.message`/`stack` stand (FFI Part 11 §7). Every boundary collection now reads its stability from its capture; the stability-contract limb of this species survives for `->` claims over foreign data a user row or getter makes on its own account. For a captured collection the capture holds the *value* still, not the *source*: a `->` row returning one is a separate claim that the source does not vary between calls, and a row that cannot make it writes `->!` (FFI Part 1 §5.4).

Species (a) permanently excludes a pure-faced `random()` or `readLine()` — a read the program observes is never unobservable. Species (b) excludes any *replayable* read — replay is exactly what at-most-once forbids. Species (c) admits a replayable read only of data a contract or a capture holds still. The slogan survives these doors amended once: **silence means nothing the program can observe.**

### 6.3 What the claim costs

A trusted claim is per-module-author accountability, the same currency as every extern type. The compiler does not verify species membership; it verifies intrinsic rows (§6.1) and believes user rows. A claims audit belongs to review, not the checker.

## 7. The sequence posture

- **`Seq` is pure by construction.** Its field is `pull: () -> Option((a, Seq(a)))` — a pure thunk, by §2.5. Every producer and combinator is pure; building a pipeline is pure; *the five strict consumers* — `fold`, `forEach`, `find`, `any`, `all` — are the only doors effects enter through, each with a linked-`->?` callback and a `?`-marked body call. An effectful step function can no longer be smuggled into a lazy pipeline at all: `Seq.unfold` with an impure producer is §4.3's unification refusal. The `memoize` landmine — memoization observably changing how many times effects run, with no type-level story — is dissolved rather than patched: the producer position demands purity, and what remains for `memoize` to claim is species (b).
- **Effectful sequences are nominal siblings, never effect parameters.** The two-point lattice degenerates "a type parameterized by effect" into "two types" — so Hexagon ships two types, on the Kotlin `Sequence`/`Flow` and F# `Seq`/`AsyncSeq` precedent, and no effect variable ever appears in a type constructor's arguments. `Stream(a)` is the pull-impure sibling: no tail, field arrow the impure constant, consumers `->!` (`stream.md`, the owning spec). A push sibling (`Observable`) is future work; async is out of scope (§1).
- **The FFI gains a declared choice at the boundary.** A foreign iterable at a `Seq(a)` position takes Part 3's launder — adaptation plus at-most-once memoization, species (b), purity manufactured honestly. The same object at a `Stream(a)` position crosses raw, protocol to protocol, no adapter and no memoization — impurity declared instead (FFI Part 3's `Stream` section). Nothing about the v1 `Seq` boundary changes.
- **The coupling that makes purity true.** Statements §6.2 (a lambda cannot touch an outer `var`) and §6.4 (no ref cells, no mutable fields) are what make a `->` face a *fact* rather than a convention — there is no mutable capture for a pure-faced closure to smuggle. That same fence is why a compiler may treat pure instantiations as free for fusion and reordering within the semantics those sections fix. The effects discipline leans on those sections; weakening them re-opens this ruling.

## 8. Emission

Colours and marks erase. Arrows, `!`, `?`, and the trusted arrows on boundary rows exist for the checker and the reader; emitted JavaScript is identical with and without them, and no runtime representation of colour exists. (The debug probe's captured sink, §6.2, is the probe's own implementation detail, not a colour representation.)

## 10. Display

Display is part of the contract: a signature a reader cannot see is not a face. Functions §5.1 owns the display grammar; this section owns what is specific to colour.

- **The trio renders everywhere a face does** — hover, diagnostics, completion detail, and the generated `.d.ts`. A TypeScript face has one function arrow, so the declaration file carries the Hexagon signature as a generated documentation line (`` Hexagon: `face` ``), merged into the author's block per Doc Comments §7.3 and emitted only where the face carries a colour — purity is the silent one (§1).
- **Distinguish, and only where the grammar cannot spell it.** A face carrying **exactly one** distinct effect variable displays it undecorated, `->?`, wherever that variable stands — except where the captured-variable paragraph below needs the decoration. A face carrying **more than one** — inference produces these; the written grammar, which links every `->?` in a signature into one colour, cannot — numbers them in order of first appearance, `->?¹`, `->?²`. Constants are never numbered: `->` and `->!` mean what they say in every position. The numbering's unit is the one displayed type expression, nested function types included. The decoration is display-only, not grammar: pasted into source it fails at the lexer.

  **A captured variable is decorated where an inlet-less `->?` at the display location would name a different owner** *(#873)*. A local face carrying an enclosing signature's variable (§2.2.1's local-face paragraph) displays it undecorated, as any single variable, wherever a written inlet-less `->?` at that location would name the same variable — which it does exactly when the nearest enclosing signature that can own a variable (§2.2.2) owns the displayed one, or has been joined to it (§3.4). The location is where the face is displayed — the hovered occurrence, the completion cursor, or the diagnostic's primary span — not where the binding was written, and the probe is taken over the settled colours, after the body holding that location has closed — at the knot's close for a `fun` block member (§3.4). Where a nearer inlet-bearing signature stands between the binding and the variable's owner, an undecorated paste would name the nearer signature's variable and join it to the captured one — a narrowing with no report of its own until something pins (§4.2) — and the display therefore **numbers** the captured variable, `->?¹`, even when it is the only variable in the face: in `outer(action: () ->? Unit)`'s body, `let g = action` hovers `g : () ->? Unit`, while the same binding inside a helper `fun mid(cb: () ->? Int)` that conducts nothing of `outer`'s hovers `g : () ->?¹ Unit`; where `mid` does conduct `action`, the two colours are one variable and the display is plain again. The numbering is this section's usual display-only licence — the grammar cannot spell `outer`'s variable from inside `mid`, and a paste fails at the lexer rather than narrowing silently — and tooling **identifies the owner** beside it: hover, completion detail, and diagnostics that show such a face name the signature whose variable it is (`->?¹` is `outer`'s colour, captured). Where a join has made one variable of two signatures' colours (§3.4), the owner line names the outermost of them; the decoration test is untouched by the join — the joined colours are one variable, so any signature that owns it names it. The probe is the inlet-less arrow, not the whole face: a displayed face that carries an inlet of its own is a signature wherever it is written (§2.2.2), so a paste of it quantifies a fresh colour that the body producing the face — or, at a use site, the expression the annotation stands over — joins to the captured one again: a faithful paste, owed no decoration. Where a face carries a captured variable that needs the decoration beside variables of its own, all are numbered in order of first appearance as elsewhere — `(() ->?¹ Int) ->?² Int` for a nested helper polymorphic in its own callback and conducting a captured one (§3.4) — and the owner line names which numbers are captured.

  The predecessor rule also numbered a *lone* variable with no inlet occurrence, because an undecorated write-back would there have taken the else-constant reading and silently meant `=>!`. With that reading abolished (§2.2.1) the case is no longer a silent one: the undecorated spelling is exactly right about the colour — one variable — and a paste into a position that cannot host it is caught by §4.4, which explains why in a sentence. **Numbering marks what the grammar cannot express, not what the checker will refuse**; a legality error with a teaching message is a better outcome than a token failure, so the lone-variable case now displays plainly. The one lone variable still numbered is the captured one above, on the opposite ground: there the grammar can spell an arrow at that location, and the arrow it spells means something else — a re-reading no legality error would catch *(#873)*.

- *(#410.)* **A constrained face wears its constraints as a bracket prefix**, `<a: Show> (Seq(a), (a) ->? Unit) ->? Unit` — Functions §5.1 owns the grammar. It is display-only under exactly this section's licence: a paste into an annotation position is refused there as numbering is refused at the lexer, and for the same reason — display marks what the grammar cannot express. With the separator gone, a displayed type contains no `=>` at any depth (§2).
- **A machine-written annotation never spells a variable colour.** What an annotation writer spells is one type torn out of its signature, and whether the fragment's new home offers an inlet is not a question the fragment answers. Both constants write freely; a machine-written impure return annotation is spelled `->!` and needs no parentheses (§2.6).

## 11. Rejected alternatives (do not re-litigate without new information)

- **Effect-parameterized types** (`Seq(a, e)`, Koka-style rows): refused — it reintroduces the machinery HM-nativeness exists to avoid, and the two-point lattice makes the nominal split (§7) strictly cheaper.
- **The Swift collapse** — weakening `!` to "possibly impure" and dropping `?`: the recorded fallback if the trichotomy had failed its bake. It failed the other way: `!` on a body's forwarded-callback call would constantify the variable and kill the pure face. Not adopted; recorded because it is the natural simplification a future reader will propose.
- **A second effect variable for const ⊔ var bodies**: never — §2.4's join.
- **The impure constant as a multi-member door's licence** *(#867)*: refused. A `widens` door whose listed members disagree on colour would publish a face no member wrote, chosen because it is the weaker promise; the door wears the member's contract or there is no door (Constraints §4.7). The body's inferred purity is refused on the same ground and a second one: a door whose colour came off its body would change colour when the body changed, which §13.3 exists to prevent.
- **Relaxing the inlet rule for a contract whose effectful callback a body could ignore** *(#867)*: refused. A member header is a signature under §2.2.1, and whether some body would leave a `->!` parameter uncalled is a fact about bodies, not headers; the linked conflict form reports a body against an otherwise legal linked signature and relaxes nothing (§13.2).
- **Marks on references** ("effectful values"): values wear no colours (§2.6); the effect happens at the call.
- **`^` or `~` as the conducting mark**: `?` won on the trained gesture — "this line defers to my caller."
- **`!->`**: not a token; the mark trails what it condemns, on arrows exactly as at calls (§2.3).
- **The `->` / `=>` / `=>!` spelling**: superseded. It distinguished constant-from-variable by the arrow *head* and variable-from-constant by the *tail* — two axes for three spellings — and the head grouping paired `=>` with `=>!` when the semantic pairing is `->` with `=>!`, both being constants. It also left `=>` serving four roles at once — lambda arrow, `match`/`catch` arm arrow, effect arrow, and the displayed scheme's constraint separator *(the fourth has since gone the same way, #410: the display is source-shaped, §10)*. The third role is the sole cause of the withdrawn return-annotation parenthesization rule (§2.6), and the third against the fourth put two meanings of one symbol in a single displayed line (§2). One arrow with the call trichotomy's own marks fixes all of it.
- **The else-constant rule**: withdrawn. It read a `=>` with nothing to link to as the impure constant, which gave one spelling two meanings selected by position — the divergence §2.5 had to say out loud — and put the arrow side out of step with the call side, where a `?` with nothing to conduct has always been an error rather than a re-reading. Its two clients, the data field and the result-only face, are better served by writing `->!`, which is what they meant. Do not restore it to spare a writer three keystrokes: the keystrokes are the point.
- **Numbering a lone inlet-less variable in the display** (§10): dropped with the else-constant rule, whose silent re-reading was its only justification. A face the grammar *can* spell displays plainly even when this position will refuse it; §4.4 explains the refusal better than a lexer failure does. The captured variable that an undecorated paste would re-own is the one lone variable numbered again, because there the paste is neither refused nor faithful — a silent dependency change, which no teaching error reaches (#873, §10).
- **A pure-asserting term lambda arrow** (`x -> body`): dropped — inference from the body plus the ascription demand covers it with zero new syntax.
- **Exact matching at the constraint seat** *(#867)*: refused. A pure instance cannot manufacture the effect a `->!` member's exactness would demand, so effectful members would be unhonorable at any in-memory type; the seat compares (§13.2) — a door's own written arrows excepted, exact as any written face is (Constraints §4.7).
- **A `widens` door showing its body's inferred colour** *(#867)*: refused. It would let a body change flip every direct caller's marks and inferred colours under an unchanged interface, and it would hang two colours on one operation (§13.3).
- **A `?` that names the nearest signature's own variable** *(#873)* — reading the mark as "conduct *my* colour" and unifying the callee's with it: refused. It would constantify nothing but would join every captured colour to the innermost header, so a nested helper would lose polymorphism in a callback it never runs. The mark reports the callee's variable and joins the body's own (§3.1).
- **Refusing a `?` on a captured callback** *(#873)* — requiring every conducted colour to arrive as the body's own parameter: refused. It would make a nested helper unable to do what the same code written as a lambda already does, and the enforcement is lexical either way; a local face is a monotype fragment of its enclosing scope (§2.2.1), not a signature that owes its own inlet.
- **The conservative-conduct rule** *(#868)* — an undetermined call inside an inlet-bearing body conducts, and an inlet-bearing body's unconstrained colour is not defaulted: withdrawn. It gave `?` a second meaning, "inference has not resolved this here", beside its one meaning, a preserved dependency; it minted a second effect variable on every closure builder — `defer : (() ->?¹ Unit) ->?² () ->?¹ Unit`, a face the grammar cannot spell and no header could correct, since a header has no outer-arrow seat — the only repair was abandoning header syntax for a binding annotation restating the whole signature — and it forced `?` between knot siblings that perform nothing. The face it guarded was never at risk: a colour nothing constrains is not a dependency, and the caller-supplied colour it feared losing is the parameter's own variable, which the defaulting never touches. Do not restore it for the knot's sake: the knot's remedy is settling at its close (§3.4), not conducting.

## 13. Effect contracts at the constraint seat *(#867)*

A constraint member header is the one function header in the language with no body beneath it to infer from, and it is therefore the one that **writes its own outer arrow**: `show(value: a) -> String`, `read(source: a) ->! String`, `run(runner: r, action: () ->? Unit) ->? Unit` (Constraints §2 owns the form; Functions §4.1's implementation headers keep `:` and keep inferring). What the header writes is a **contract** — what a caller may assume, and what an instance must satisfy — and this section fixes how the two sides meet. Every rule elsewhere in this document stands; §13 adds one seat and says exactly what happens there.

### 13.1 Two levels

- **An instance is an implementation, and its colour is inferred.** An `honor` member body, a default body, and a `widens` door body solve their own colour by §3.4's three arms, exactly as any body does. The header such a body hangs under writes `:` where it writes anything and never an outer arrow (Functions §4.1; Constraints §4.1, §4.7). Of one constraint's instances some are pure and some are not, and nothing written at the constraint says which.
- **The contract never learns the instance's colour.** It knows only what it refuses: a pure contract cannot be met by an impure instance. Reading a contract tells a caller the most an instance may do, never what a given instance does.
- **So every arrow a contract writes is a bound, and the seat is a comparison, not a unification.** An arrow the caller invokes — the outer arrow first of all — is a ceiling: `->!` there means *an instance may perform effects*, and a pure body beneath it is not §4.2's lie, because the header is not that body's face. An arrow the caller supplies — a callback's — is a floor: the instance must place no stronger purity requirement on it than the contract does — accept at least what the contract promises (§13.2). This is the one seat where §1's "colours unify or they do not" gives way to an ordering, and it is the whole of the exception: at calls (§4.1), at demands and fields (§4.3), and on every implementation's own written face (§4.2), the arrows are exact.

### 13.2 The seat check

In one sentence: **an instance accepts everything its contract promises to accept, and performs no more effects than its contract permits.** The mechanism is Constraints §4.1's checking posture with the effect slot compared instead of unified — no search, no second reading, one committed answer per seat:

- **The contract is instantiated at each colour its variable can take** — once, if the header writes no `->?`; twice, pure and impure, if it does (§13.4).
- **The body is inferred over the recoloured shape, then compared.** The contract's *types* reach the body as Constraints §4.1's expected type (Functions §4.3) with **every effect slot replaced by a fresh variable of the body's own**, one per slot — the one place §3.4's expected-types bullet is qualified: an expectation's colours do not arrive here, because the seat is about to compare them. At the seat §3.4's conduit arm is qualified as its expected-types bullet is: a call whose callee's outermost colour is one of these fresh variables, or a colour the ordering carries such a slot to — a parameter's, a local's that carries it, or a closure's that conducts it, the test read against the ordering as it then stands, which only grows, so a knot's close reaches its fixpoint as §3.4's arms do — does **not** join that colour to the colour of the body the call stands in — it imposes that colour as a **lower bound** on it, a body that calls what it is handed being at least as effectful as what it is handed — and the body the call stands in is the instance body's own, or a nested lambda's or local function's where the call stands there (§3.4's frames are unchanged: a call inside a closure the body merely constructs bounds that closure's colour, not the body's). So the walk's bounds on the callback and on the outer arrow meet on the body's colour without making the two slots one variable, and the bounds test reads the ordering through: a slot bounded below by the impure constant bounds below it, in turn, every colour the ordering carries it to. Two **linked** slots one body conducts are still made one colour — unified with each other at the second `?` call, whichever frames the two calls stand in, both slots being the member's one variable, as §3.4 makes two absorbed variables one — and a body that runs neither may keep two, displayed as §10 displays any inferred face. The body then infers as any body does (§3.4), and the **same inferred monotype** — its colours the body's own variables, never copies — is compared against each instantiation of the contract; the body's defaulting and generalization follow the seat rather than precede it (the settling bullet below; §3.4's seat clause), which is why a colour the seat settles is the colour the body's marks then read. The freshening is comparison scaffolding, and it **manufactures no inlet**: the body's inlet is the contract's, decided by the header's own written `->?` parameters (§2.2.1, §13.4) and by nothing the freshening mints — so a `->` callback parameter's calls stay bare off §3.4's defaulting, the join below finding nothing, wherever the body has not itself fixed the slot — the join is the answer of the disposal below, and ordinary unification in the body precedes it — a `->!` callback parameter's calls wear `!` once the seat has settled its colour, and a `->?` callback parameter's calls wear `?` off the slot the seat leaves a variable (below). For a `widens` door the scheme compared is the member's **derived restriction** (Constraints §4.7) — the door at the member's own seats — since the door's wider seats are not the contract's; the door's own written signature is its face, checked by §4.2 as any written face is. A door is freshened at no slot: its written signature fixes every nested colour as a face (§4.2), only its outer colour is inferred, and the seat compares that face, restricted, against the contract; a `->?` a door writes at a widened seat denotes the member's own variable, not a second one — the door's face is the contract's arrows over the door's wider seats, so the two instantiations instantiate that one variable and the walk meets no free body colour at any supplied arrow, which is why the settling bullet below has nothing to settle at a door, and nothing to conflict either: with no bounds, a door's effect at a written `->!` parameter is §3.4's source arm, reported in the base form at the offending call, never in a conflict form — and a door's seat report places as a member's does, at the offending call in the door's body.
- **The two are walked together, and colours are ordered, pure below impure, at each arrow's sign.** The sign is the **product** the variance analysis already computes (`decisions-ml-dialect-generalization-2026-08.md` §5 — borrowed here as the direction of an ordering, its one client outside the generalization question that document's §5.4 says it answers; §3.4's occurrence walk counts the effect slot at it): the walk starts covariant at the outer arrow; descending into a parameter flips the sign, descending into a result, a tuple component, a record field, or a union payload keeps it, and descending into a constructor's argument multiplies by that constructor's variance — inferred for a transparent type, declared for an opaque one, the compiler-side table for a compiler-known one. Where the product is **covariant** the caller invokes the arrow and the body's colour must be at most the contract's; where it is **contravariant** the caller supplies it and the contract's colour must be at most the body's; where it is **invariant** the colours must be equal — a ceiling and a floor at once, both of the contract's own colour; and where the analysis's **unused** point erases the occurrence, the slot is compared with nothing — nothing the contract writes beneath a constructor parameter no value of that constructor carries reaches a value, wherever in the member's type that constructor stands — and the disposal releases it; a `->?` whose only occurrence inside a parameter type stands there is a vacuous inlet — the header's own outer `->?` sharing that vacuity, a spine arrow's colour being no inlet of its own (§2.2.1) — legal under §2.2.1's coarse test: a phantom position contributes no bound and establishes no execution dependency by its occurrence there — the member's variable stays the member's, polymorphic, instantiated fresh at each call and pinned by nothing at that position — so nothing about the occurrence pins a caller-supplied variable pure, and a call whose colour it is marks as §4.1 marks any variable colour, by whatever else pins it. One sign per arrow, so `make: (Int) -> (() ->? Unit)` in a parameter is contravariant at both its arrows — reached through a result, but of a supplied function. Non-effect structure checks as Constraints §4.1 already checks it.
- **A body colour still a variable collects the bounds the walk imposes**, over the whole walk at one instantiation of the contract, and the seat passes when every variable's bounds are consistent — on a two-point lattice, the ordering propagated once and then one test per variable. The pure instantiation is compared first and the impure second; a failure at the pure instantiation is the seat's report and the impure comparison is not made, so an instance is told what its pure obligation costs before what its impure one does. A colour on either side that traces to a §4.4 recovery is compared with nothing: the recovery binds nothing it meets, at this seat as anywhere. A failed seat names the arrow that failed and the direction it failed in. Which of §9's contract rows reports, and in which form, is decided by the **failing arrow's** contract colour and direction, whatever the outer arrow — the selection table below is normative for it, and §9 holds every row's base text. What this bullet owns is the **position form**: every frame names the arrow's actual position — the flat form at the outer arrow; the result form, "the function this instance returns …", where the failing arrow is the contract's result reached by result steps alone (descent into a tuple, record, or union payload, or a constructor's argument, ends that path); otherwise "a function this instance supplies …" naming the top-level contract parameter the arrow stands under, "inside the parameter `k`", or "inside its result" where the path descends through the result before it turns — every arrow but the outer one stands under a named parameter or the result, so every frame names a position; the narrower-acceptance rows take the same three forms — at a top-level parameter, beneath one, through the result (§9); and every row's advice names the same arrow its frame does, in the same position form — "write `->!` on the member" at the outer arrow, "write `->!` on the arrow the contract returns" where the path is result steps alone, "write `->!` on that arrow inside the parameter `k`" beneath a parameter, "write `->!` on that arrow inside its result" where the path descends through the result before it turns, and for the narrower-acceptance rows "write the member's callback parameter `->`" at a top-level parameter, "write that arrow `->` inside the parameter `k`" or "inside its result" beneath one — advice that named the outer arrow at a nested failure would send the writer to an arrow that is not the one that failed (§9). The refusal is the seat's even when its span lies inside the body: a body that hands the contract's callback to a `->` demand infers cleanly, the callback's fresh colour pinned pure by ordinary unification (§2.1), and it is the seat — never §4.3's demand row — that refuses it, reporting at the pin that fixed the colour, narrowed or raised — the demand, annotation, supplied argument, or other expression whose unification fixed it, directly or through a colour the ordering carries to it, a `->` demand met by a lambda that conducts the callback narrowing it as surely as one met by the callback itself, a `->!` demand raising an invariant `->` slot placing there likewise — because that is where the fixing happened. A failed seat settles nothing, but it holds back only mark reports: the body's defaulting and generalization then run as they would outside a seat (§3.4), so the binding still has a scheme and the body's other calls still typecheck — only the colour the seat condemned draws no mark report of its own (§4.1), a mark read off a refused colour restating the refusal in the wrong words — and no mark report is made against any colour the freshening minted, nor against any colour the ordering carries a freshened slot to — a failed seat settles none of them, so the set is the fresh variables, one per contract effect slot, together with their **forward reach** through the ordering as it stands at the failure, a colour ordinary unification has identified with any colour in that set being that colour, the identity read through pruning wherever the set is consulted — never off the variable as it stood when the edge was recorded — as the bounds test reads it: a helper that calls the handed callback, `let one(): Unit = b!()`, carries the seat's unsettled colour, and a mark read off it — "`one` wants no mark, not `!`" at a `one!()` — is no trustworthy ground for a correction, the deletion it offers damaging a body the seat's one refusal already names; a helper's colour the ordering does not reach keeps its own mark report, its error being its own; this is diagnostic recovery only — the seat still settles nothing, and the body's defaulting and generalization still run — and a genuinely wrong mark on a reached helper surfaces on the next compile, once the seat is repaired, which the conformance suite is to pin beside the absence of the deletion at `one!()` — and separately, at the slot itself, a `b!()` written correctly under a `->!` callback whose settling the seat never reached is the writer's compliance, not an error: the one refusal is the ruling, and a wrong mark on such a call surfaces on the next compile, which the conformance suite is to pin as a fact rather than inherit as a side effect.
- **A consistent seat then settles what the contract fixed.** A fresh body colour that carries an **unconditional** impure lower bound — one imposed by a supplied or invariant arrow whose contract colour is the impure constant, directly or carried to it through another variable by the ordering, a bound that holds at every instantiation — is settled to the impure constant once every instantiation has been compared and each instantiation's bounds are consistent at that instantiation — bounds are collected and tested per instantiation and never pooled across them, a linked arrow's pure upper bound at the pure instantiation beside its impure lower bound at the impure being the everyday pair pooling would read as a contradiction; the settle is once per seat, after the last comparison and never between two, so neither instantiation meets a colour the other's bounds fixed — before the body's defaulting (§3.4) and before its call marks are validated (§4.1): the contract promised the body an effectful callback, so a body that calls it writes `!`, and left a variable the colour would default pure and no body that calls what it is handed could honor the member. A variable settles whenever *some* lower bound on it comes from an arrow whose contract colour is the impure constant, whatever other **lower** bounds it carries — a **pure** upper bound beside such a lower bound is not a settle but the conflict the next bullet reports; a variable whose only impure lower bounds come from linked arrows never settles — a linked arrow imposes no impure bound at the pure instantiation, and the pure instantiation must still pass — so it stays a variable, the body's own, standing where the header's `->?` inlet stands (§13.4). A variable the rule leaves unsettled keeps its variable where any slot it stands at asks for that, and is released otherwise — the **disposal** — one variable can be two slots, ordinary unification in the body merging a parameter slot with a result slot, as `make(k) = k` does: a supplied slot whose contract colour is the member's variable keeps it a variable — the body's own signature's, protected and read exactly as any signature parameter's variable is, by §3.4's never-default rule and §4.1's variable clause, so a `->?` callback parameter's calls wear `?`, standing where the header's `->?` inlet stands; failing that, it is **released** — an invoked slot asks for no disposal, a ceiling fixing nothing, and a supplied `->` slot asks for none either, a floor at the bottom of the lattice fixing nothing, while a supplied `->!` slot has already been settled by the rule above; an **invariant** slot is compared by the sign bullet's equality and asks for nothing beyond it — its contract's impure constant has settled it by the rule above; its pure constant bounds it pure on both sides, so the disposal releases it and it defaults pure (§3.4), an invariant `->` slot admitting no raise, invariance forbidding the widening; and its member's variable is the body's own, kept exactly as a supplied linked slot keeps it, so a `->?` reaching an invariant position — `cells: Array(() ->? For a constant outer arrow the rule is a table:

| Body solves to | `->` contract | `->!` contract |
|---|---|---|
| pure | accepted | accepted |
| impure | refused | accepted |

The refused cell is Constraints §2's standing sentence — member bodies under a pure contract solve pure — given its mechanism, reported in §4.2's pure-face frame with the contract named as such, at the offending call, the contract's failing arrow a related location. The lower-right cell is what the ordering buys: a `->!` member honored at an in-memory type by a body that reads nothing.

For a failing arrow, selection is a table too — normative for the row and the form; the subject, advice, primary, and related location stand with the row in §9. Which arrow fails, and whether a direct failure pre-empts a bounds conflict, is decided before this table: walk order, direct failures first (above). A narrower-acceptance row is reached by a demand, an explicit annotation, or — where the merged colour meets no pure upper arrow the contract writes, directly or through the ordering — a merge; a merge's pure constant reports as the conflict rows where such an arrow is met. Three failing-arrow and contract-colour pairs cannot fail and have no row — an invoked `->!` (the ceiling is the top), a supplied `->` (the floor is the bottom), an unused slot (compared with nothing) — and at an invariant arrow neither a `->` below nor a `->!` above, the lattice having no room. The two selectors nest as the conflict bullet says: the conflicting variable first, then the arrow within it.

| Failing arrow's direction | Contract colour there | Failure | Row and form |
|---|---|---|---|
| invoked | `->` | above — a direct failure, the body having sourced the constant | pure-contract row, base form |
| invoked | `->` | above — a bounds conflict with a handed callback's bound | pure-contract row, conflict form |
| invoked | member's variable | above at the pure instantiation — a direct failure | linked-contract row, base form |
| invoked | member's variable | above at the pure instantiation — a bounds conflict with a handed callback's bound | linked-contract row, conflict form |
| supplied | member's variable | below at the impure instantiation — a direct failure | linked narrower-acceptance row, base form — its merge form where the pin is a merge |

The supplied direction is what keeps callbacks honest. A contract accepting either colour of callback — `action: () ->? Unit` — cannot be honored by a body that accepts only a pure one (a body handing the callback to a `->` demand, say): at the contract's impure instantiation `->!` is not at most the body's `->`. The reverse widening is free at a supplied arrow — a body may accept more than its contract promises there, and §13.4's worked example is one. Compatibility is never "pure-for-effectful at every nested arrow"; it is the sign, read once.

### 13.3 Every spelling wears the contract's mark

**A call resolved through a member marks by the contract's outer arrow at this instantiation, never by the instance's body** — the bare member call, the qualified call, the dot call (Method Syntax §7), a call at a known concrete instance, a call through evidence under a bound, and a call to a `widens` door (Constraints §4.7; Modules §5.3; Method Syntax §6.1) alike; references to any of them carry the contract's callable type (§2.6 — references are colourless). Knowing which instance answers grants no purer face: `Path.read!(p)` wears `!` at an instance whose body is pure, exactly as `source.read!()` does under `a: Readable`.

- **The door is no exception.** A `widens` binding shows the operation's widest *argument* face and the member's *contract* colour — one operation wearing two widths, never two colours. Its declaration's header writes `:` and its body is inferred and compared at the seat like any instance's, through the member's derived restriction (§13.2); the listed members of a multi-member door must agree on their contracts' linked relationship — which positions share the member's variable — and on the constants elsewhere, or the declaration is refused at its head (Constraints §4.7). The alternative is recorded in §11; what this costs is stated once, below.
- **What `!` means, restated.** A bare call is statically pure. A `!` call *may* perform effects — the contract grants no purity — and it does not promise that an effect occurs on this execution: a conditional body, or a pure body under an effectful contract, may perform none. A `?` call carries a variable in lexical scope (§3.1). Mark checking stays exact (§4.1): the required mark is the contract's arrow, and any other mark is the error it always was.
- **The price.** A pure body behind an effectful contract cannot be used as pure *through the member or its door*. An author who wants a pure operation available names one — an ordinary function beside the instance — and the compiler never exposes one on the author's behalf.

The churn this buys, under a `->!` contract:

| Body change | Calls through the member and its door | Refused alternative (door shows the body's colour) |
|---|---|---|
| pure → effectful | stay `!` | direct calls change from bare to `!`, and their callers' colours may follow |
| effectful → pure | stay `!` | direct calls change from `!` to bare, likewise |

Changing an instance within its contract's allowance changes no caller.

### 13.4 One effect variable per member

A member header is a **signature** in §2.2.1's sense: every `->?` it writes denotes one implicitly quantified effect variable, the member's own, and the inlet rule applies unchanged — an outer-only member face, `read(source: a) ->? String`, is §4.4's inlet-less refusal. The variable is quantified **at the member and instantiated fresh at every call**; it is not fixed per instance. This is the one member-level quantifier the language admits — Constraints §2's ban on member type parameters stands (§9.6 there), and this variable is not a door to them.

An instance must satisfy the contract at *both* instantiations (§13.2). For `run(runner: r, action: () ->? Unit) ->? Unit`:

| Callback supplied | What the instance may do |
|---|---|
| pure | nothing the world observes |
| impure | anything, or nothing |

Calling the callback, calling it conditionally, and ignoring it all satisfy the contract (ignoring it may break a behavioural law, which is the constraint author's business, not the checker's). An honoring body writes no arrow and needs none:

```
honor Runner<Job> =
    run(job, action) = action?()
```

The body's signature is the contract's shape with fresh colours (§13.2), so the contract's callback parameter is the body's inlet (§2.2.1) and `action?()` is §3.1's ordinary conduct — the `?` names the body's own variable, which the seat then compares. A **default body** is the same case checked once in the constraint's generic context (Constraints §2): it calls its sibling members at *their* contracts' marks and is compared against its *own* contract like any body — a default under `->` cannot call a `->!` sibling (a source under a pure contract), a default under `->?` may conduct a `->?` sibling but not call a `->!` one, and a default under `->!` may call anything. A body that performs its own unconditional effect fails the pure instantiation: its outer colour is the join (§2.4), and `->!` is not at most `->`. A body that accepts only pure callbacks fails the supplied direction (§13.2). A contract that wants both — a linked callback *and* a licence for its own effects — writes the join on its outer arrow, `run(transaction: t, action: () ->? Unit) ->! Unit`: §2.4's `withTransaction` shape as a contract, whose calls always wear `!`.

### 13.5 The unmarkable forms

§5's four forms stay pure, and the ground moves from a rule about every constraint to a fact about the prelude's: operators, `for` heads, and interpolation elaborate to prelude members by identity — brackets to a compiler-known lookup, `Vector`'s and `String`'s `at` or `Map`'s keyed read, each pure (Collections Part 3 §5, §9; Collections Part 4 §4.1) — and **every member of every prelude constraint writes `->` on its outer arrow** — what the four forms need — and, as standard-library policy, on every arrow beneath it: no prelude member is effect-polymorphic (Constraints §7 lists them; the focused owners list the rest). That is a compile-breaking standard-library constraint, which the conformance suite is to pin alongside the prelude seat order. A user constraint's effectful member reaches no unmarkable form, because no unmarkable form reaches a user constraint. `Iterable` therefore still admits no effectful instance and `Stream` still has no instance (`stream.md` §4.5); a pattern's `view` is §4.3's demand, unchanged (Pattern Declarations §2.1).

### 13.6 Evidence, emission, display

- **A dictionary is a value.** Instance construction is evaluation-free (Constraints §6.3), and an effectful member's effect happens at the call — where its mark is — never at the dictionary. Nothing about evidence, sharing, or CSE reads a member's colour.
- **§8 stands.** The seat holds the body whatever its colour, and colours erase.
- **A member's face displays its contract arrows** (§10), and the `.d.ts` dictionary interface carries a coloured member's Hexagon face on the documentation line §10 already emits (FFI Part 9 §2.2).
